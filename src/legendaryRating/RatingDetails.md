# Design Goal

Depending on the kind of item we're rating, different stats may or may not actually be useful. This isn't always immediately obvious, so our goal here is twofold:

1. Adjust our overall rating according to how useful the stats are that have actually rolled
2. Use the "details" section to tell the user about all the details about what has contributed, in bullet point format, ordered from strongest benefit to strongest detraction

# Rating instructions
- Stats that are identified as top tier or are specifically called out as the most desirable stats for an archetype should contribute much more significantly than other stats that are called out as useful.
- Even "bad" rolls on really desired stats should give a solid overall rating. Getting the right stat rolls is often far harder than getting "good" % rolls on those stats.
- One solid roll (75% or more) on a really desired stat should net the item at least an "Excellent" overall rating.
- Two solid rolls on two really desired stats should net the item a "Godly" overall rating.

# Details section instructions
- Use bullet point format
- Order from strongest benefit to strongest detraction
- Don't try to show the user numbers or percentages in relation to how things are affecting ratings
- The key here is we're trying to identify the impactful considerations, as well as call out any particular nuances that apply to the specific item

# Archetypes

If a particular roll is not noted, it should be considered relatively unhelpful.

## 1h Weapons
These items are generally defined as using 4ap per attack and thus able to attack often.

In general (unless otherwise noted), increased damage is the most desired roll, followed very closely by ignore armor. Skill fatigue reduction is a bit more valuable because multiple attacks per turn will get more use out of it. Flat fatigue reduction is better than nothing, but almost never particularly desired compared to the other rolls you could have gotten instead.

- Daggers (`Dagger`): Most daggers do not benefit at all from ignore armor, because their primary use is the Puncture skill which ignores armor altogether. Raw damage and skill use fatigue reduction are the desired rolls. Flat fatigue reduction is basically worthless on all daggers, since they are already so light.
    - `Qatal Dagger`: A specific type of dagger that does benefit from ignore armor. Raw damage and ignore armor are top tier, followed by skill use fatigue reduction. With good damage and ignore armor rolls, they can be one of the very best 1h weapons in the game.
- Axes (`Hand Axe`): Top tier rolls are raw damage and ignore armor; armor damage is serviceable. Damage to shield is rarely useful. 
- Whips (`Battle Whip`): Whips are unique in that they are most commonly used not to deal damage, but to disarm opponents. Thus, for whips, reduced fatigue on skill use is actually the top tier roll. After that, damage is desired. Ignore armor is much less useful than with most other weapons.
- Cleavers (`Cleaver`, `Khopesh`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls.
- Flails (`Flail`, `Three-headed Flail`): While the standard damage followed by ignore armor certainly applies to flails, the fatigue reduction on skill use does come into play a bit more here, since the baseline fatigue use of flail skills is higher than most other weapons.
- Hammers (`Warhammer`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Armor damage is slightly more valuable than with other weapons, given that the hammer is often used to counter heavy armor (but raw damage intrinsically does more armor damage, and ignore armor just kills enemies inside their armor--leaving it for you to loot). With good damage and ignore armor rolls, they can be one of the very best 1h weapons in the game.
- Maces (`Mace`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. With good damage and ignore armor rolls, they can be one of the very best 1h weapons in the game.
- Spears (`Spear`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Even an excellently rolled spear may not find much use in the late game, due to the lack of significant damage and ignore armor. However, in the early game a well rolled spear can be a game defining find.
- Swords (`Fencing Sword`, `Shamshir`, `Sword`): Damage followed closely by ignore armor are top tier. However, for fencing sword specifically, the fatigue reduction on skill use has a bit more merit, because fatigue buildup reduces initiative and its unique skill `Lunge` scales damage off of initiative (even so, damage and ignore armor are still better rolls on it).
- Goblin 1h weaponry (`Goblin Skewer`, `Cruel Falchion`): While damage and ignore armor are desirable, even the best rolled named version of them might only be marginally better than the human alternative. Generally considered not worthy of wielding.
- Orc 1h weaponry (`Orc Axe`, `Orc Cleaver`): Damage followed closely by ignore armor are the top tier rolls. Fatigue reduction on skill use is ranked a bit higher than it normally would be on the human alternatives, to make up for the extra fatigue on skill use that the orc weapons have baseline (although still not nearly as desirable as damage and ignore armor).

## 2h Weapons
- Axes (`Bardiche`, `Greataxe`, `Heavy Rusty Axe`, `Longaxe`): All follow the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Increased shield damage is often considered a wasted roll, and is only very rarely useful. For longaxe and its potential roll for increased head chance, it is actually not a negative if paired with increased damage or ignore armor, as they actually make headshots very potent (otherwise, for most items it's an unwanted roll as it splits armor damage between helmet and body armor).
- Cleavers (`Crypt Cleaver`, `Rusty Warblade`, `Two-Handed Scimitar`): All follow the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Increased shield damage is always considered a wasted roll for these weapons (as they are not generally used to break shields).
- Flails (`Two-Handed Flail`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Increased head chance is actually somewhat desirable on the two-handed flail given its very decent single-shot damage output (although not as desirable as a damage or ignore armor roll).
- Hammers (`Polehammer`, `Two-Handed Hammer`, `Two-Handed Skull Hammer`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Armor damage is slightly more valuable than with other weapons, given that the hammer is often used to counter heavy armor (but raw damage intrinsically does more armor damage, and ignore armor just kills enemies inside their armor--leaving it for you to loot). Increased shield damage is always considered a wasted roll for these weapons. Increased headshot chance for the polehammer is decent, but not as desirable as damage or ignore armor.
- Maces (`Polemace`, `Two-Handed Mace`, `Two-Handed Spiked mace`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Increased shield damage is always considered a wasted roll for these weapons. Increased headshot chance for the polemace is decent, but not as desirable as damage or ignore armor.
- Polearms (`Billhook`, `Bladed Pike`, `Jagged Pike`, `Pike`, `Swordlance`, `Warscythe`, `Spetum`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. However, there are unique situations among these weapons:
    - `Spetum`: is basically never used. A billhook is superior in every applicable way. Even so, I suppose if you found a solid named Spetum with damage and ignore armor rolls, there's a chance you might use it.
    - `Warscythe` and `Swordlance`: slightly benefit more than normal from reduced fatigue on skill use, since the Reap skill is very fatigue intense. Increased headshot chance is generally not desired.
    - `Bladed Pike`, `Jagged Pike`, `Pike`: Increased headshot chance is generally not desired.
    - `Billhook` does enough damage that increased headshot chance is slightly beneficial, especially if paired with a roll of increased damage or ignore armor.
- Swords (`Greatsword`, `Warbrand`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Increased shield damage is always considered a wasted roll for these weapons. Increased headshot chance is decent, but not as desirable as damage or ignore armor.

### Ranged Weapons
- Crossbows (`Crossbow`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls.
- Throwing weapons (`Javelins`, `Throwing Axes`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Armor damage is also applicable, but not as desirable as damage and ignore armor. Named throwing weapons are in a bit of a weird spot because the "heavy javelin" and "heavy throwing axe" can easily outclass a named non-heavy variant that hasn't rolled well. Minimum damage rolls above 35 for javelins and 30 for axes, or armor penetration above 45 for javelins and 30 for axes are needed to be better than a simple "heavy" javelin/axe. Chance to hit head is generally not desired, because it is more likely to split armor damage between helmet and body armor. Additional ammo is better than nothing, but without increased damage or ignore armor the piece will remain unused. Additional accuracy is generally considered a waste of a roll because throwing weapons are already rather accurate at their effective range. Reduced fatigue is basically a waste of a roll.
- Firearms (`Handgonne`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Armor damage is also applicable, but not as desirable as damage and ignore armor. Increased accuracy is better than nothing. Reduced fatigue is basically a waste of a roll.
- Bows (`Warbow`): Follows the standard of damage followed by ignore armor as top tier rolls. Armor damage is also applicable, but not as desirable as damage and ignore armor. Bow are unique among ranged weapons where reduced fatigue on skill use is somewhat applicable given the amount of fatigue the `quick shot` skill can build up. Also unique to bows is that additional accuracy is somewhat applicable as well, given that they are often doing damage at range and thus need better accuracy.
    - `Boondock Bow`: This goblin bow unfortunately follows the same pattern as other goblin weaponry: a top-rolled named piece is simply not as good as an unnamed "human/baseline" equivalent (the warbow in this case).

### Shields
- Baseline Shields: In general, melee defense is the top roll, followed a bit by ranged defense. After that, increased durability is in a shield quite useful for being able to take more hits. Reduced fatigue is better than the baseline archetype, but largely considered a waste of a roll. The `Metal Heater Shield`, `Golden Round Shield`, and `Named Sipar Shield` are considered the best in slot shields.
- Undead shields: Can be excellent shields, but their lack of durability means they are often destroyed by an axe-wielding foe. Generally not as desirable as normal shields.
- Orc shields: Almost never used due to the immense fatigue cost and their defensive stats not really being better than unnamed baseline shields. Useful as a payday and not much else.

### Armor (and Helmets)
Armor (and Helmets) are a little unique for named items because they always get the same rolls: Durability and reduced fatigue. Because of this, what makes a great item is not which rolls they get, but which of the rolls got a good percentile. Increased durability is hands down the most distinguishing attribute of named armor and helmets--a top percentile roll means it's a top tier piece. Reduced fatigue is always a nice to have, but does not define the piece like durability does.

That said, there is one more significant aspect to armor that defines it: the archetypes have their own ranges on the possible durability and fatigue rolls, and that can often define what exact piece is best in slot. To make this more nuanced there are three general categories to armor:
- Light armor, used with the `nimble` perk. Defined by fatigue less than 16 for armor and less than 10 for helmets.
- Heavy armor, used with the `battleforged` perk. Defined by durability greater than ~240 for armor and greater than ~200 for helmets
- Medium armor, sometimes used by "nimbleforged" builds; a combination of picking both nimble and battleforged. This is a unique region that named items can sometimes fall in, where fatigue is near or within the "light armor" range but durability is still very respectable (~170+ for armor and ~150+ for helmets). This is a good option to call out when a piece falls into this category (it may very well also fall into the light or heavy armor categories as well).
